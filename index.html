How it works

Plan once. Check off. Print when they ask.

HomeSchool Scribe turns the thing you already do every morning, checking off lessons, into the records your state, an evaluator, or a college will ask for later.

Plan a term in one tap

Add a course, choose the days, and HomeSchool Scribe lays out the lessons around your breaks. Photograph or paste a book’s contents page and it becomes a term.

Records that fill themselves in

Check a lesson and the child is marked present, the minutes are logged as hours, and work to be graded goes on Your desk. A field trip for all four children is one entry. Every child on one screen; kids get their own list.

Paperwork, ready

Attendance reports, report cards, transcripts with GPA, portfolios, and your state's filings, as PDFs, with reminders before they're due.

Records

Built from what you already checked off.

Every document a homeschool gets asked for, drawn from the same check marks. Print any of them the day someone asks.

  • Attendance and hours. Days, hours, monthly totals, and a signed certification page.
  • Report cards and transcripts. Report cards your way: points, weighted categories, pass/fail, or narrative. The high school transcript has a section of its own, right below.
  • Portfolio. Photos of work filed by student, course, and date, printed with their captions.
  • State filings. Notice letters, progress assessments, and the documents each filing asks for, assembled from your records.
  • Your own copies, kept for you. Photos, every report you generate, and a dated backup land in Files as ordinary files, every time you open the app. Point them at iCloud Drive if you like.

High school transcript

Four years of high school, kept as you go.

Every high-school course you grade feeds the transcript. By the time someone asks for it, it's already written: no rebuilding high school from a shoebox in senior year.

  • Every high-school year. One section per school year, each with a year summary.
  • Courses and grades. High-school courses are graded, by you, and every one feeds the transcript.
  • Credits. On every course, and on the transcript.
  • Weighted and unweighted GPA. Both, on the same page.
  • Test scores and activities. They print with the courses.
  • Years before the app. They go in as finished courses, one line each: name, level, credits, a letter grade.
  • A PDF, the day they ask. And a copy of every report you generate is kept in Files.

Student Mode

Kids get their own list.

Hand over the iPad and each child sees only their day: big checkboxes, a timer for the lesson they're on, and a button that tells you when they're stuck. A parent PIN keeps the rest of the app closed.

Their check marks become your attendance and hours, and the timer feeds the hours log, so the record is kept without anyone keeping it.

Privacy

Your family's records stay in your iCloud.

HomeSchool Scribe has no accounts and no servers. Your records sync between your devices through your own iCloud account, and the app never sends your records to us. It has no analytics and shows no ads, and it never sees names or your children's information. Student Mode collects nothing about your children.

Progress summaries are drafted on your iPhone from the report card's own numbers, and you edit them before they print; nothing is sent anywhere. Your copies are ordinary files in Files, not a format only this app can open.

Made by a homeschooler

I was homeschooled. Every summer, before the school year started, my mom spent weeks at the kitchen table writing out the whole year by hand, one child at a time: a folder for each of us, a page for each subject, every assignment with its page numbers and the problems to do. Each morning I opened my folder and checked things off. She never complained, but I saw the weeks it took, and I saw her do it again the next summer.

HomeSchool Scribe is that folder, and those weeks, in your pocket. Add a course, pick the days, and the year lays itself out. Check off the day and the records write themselves, so the weeks she spent on paper can go back to your kids. It's built in Villa Rica, Georgia, by a homeschool graduate who wanted every homeschool mom to have it a little easier than mine did. We read every message and answer every one. Taking good care of the families who use HomeSchool Scribe matters to us as much as the app itself.
