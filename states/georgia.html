Homeschool requirements · Georgia

Homeschooling in Georgia

Moderate paperwork · A notice and a yearly assessment or records check.

Submit a Declaration of Intent to the state each year, teach 180 days of at least 4.5 hours, write an annual progress report, and test every three years starting in grade 3.

What Georgia asks

NoticeAnnual Declaration of Intent submitted online to the Georgia Department of Education within 30 days of starting and by September 1 each year.
Days and hours180 days, 810 hours a year, 4.5 hours a day
SubjectsReading, Language Arts, Math, Social Studies, Science
AssessmentNationally standardized test at least every three years beginning at the end of grade 3 (results kept, not submitted). Annual written progress assessment kept for three years.
Records to keepAttendance records and annual progress reports, retained for three years.
Parent qualificationHigh school diploma or GED.
Worth knowingMonthly attendance forms are no longer submitted to the state; keep them in your own files.

What HomeSchool Scribe does in Georgia

  • Declaration of Intent: Prepares the notice letter and the course of study; reminds you 14 days and 2 days before; opens the portal and keeps your confirmation.
  • Annual progress assessment: Prepares the report card and the attendance and hours report; reminds you 14 days and 2 days before; keeps it in records.
  • Standardized test (every 3 years from grade 3): Prepares the transcript; reminds you 14 days and 2 days before; keeps it in records.

Attendance, hours, and grades fill in from the lessons you check off, so the documents above come from records you already have. Records are kept for 3 years, the period Georgia asks for.
