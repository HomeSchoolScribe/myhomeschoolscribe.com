Homeschool requirements · New Hampshire

Homeschooling in New Hampshire

No filings · Nothing to send; keep records for yourself and for college.

Since July 2026 New Hampshire asks for no notice, no evaluation and no records. A short declaration goes to the district only if you are taking a child out of a public school, or you want to use public school programs.

What New Hampshire asks

NoticeNone in most cases. A declaration of home education is needed only when withdrawing a child from a public school, or to access public school programs under RSA 193:1-c.
Days and hoursNew Hampshire sets no number. HomeSchool Scribe plans against 180 days unless you change it.
SubjectsNot specified
AssessmentNone required.
Records to keepNone required. Many families keep a portfolio and a yearly evaluation anyway, as their own account of the year rather than anyone else's.
Parent qualificationNone.
Worth knowingThe Home Education Freedom Act (HB 1268, Chapter 297) rewrote RSA 193-A in July 2026. It repealed the recordkeeping and evaluation section, removed the required subject list, and made the declaration optional. 180 days is a planning default only.

Records · Keep in your files		By the end of the school yearKeep in your filesSince the Home Education Freedom Act took effect on July 1, 2026, New Hampshire asks for no notice, no evaluation, no portfolio and no records. RSA 193-A:6, the recordkeeping and evaluation section, was repealed along with RSA 193-A:10 and :11, and the declaration of home education became optional. The Department of Education says notifications for starting or ending a program are no longer required and that it will not accept, keep or acknowledge them. A declaration still comes up in two situations, withdrawing a child from a public school and using public school courses or activities under RSA 193:1-c; the statute names no recipient and sets no deadline for it, and local school boards write their own policy for RSA 193:1-c participation, so the school or district involved is the one to ask. New Hampshire sets no deadline for any of this, so no reminders are set. Families often keep attendance, a course of study and a transcript anyway: the state issues no diploma, a home educated student self-certifies high school completion for federal student aid, and a New Hampshire public school principal reviews a transferring student's prior work before awarding credit. A parent may also sign an optional certificate of completion, and the commissioner issues one within 30 days if it is sent in.
