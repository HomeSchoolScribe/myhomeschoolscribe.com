Homeschool requirements · Minnesota

Homeschooling in Minnesota

Moderate paperwork · A notice and a yearly assessment or records check.

Submit a letter of intent by October 1 each year, teach the required subjects, and give an annual nationally normed test (results kept by the family).

What Minnesota asks

NoticeAnnual letter of intent (full report the first year, short form after) to the local superintendent by October 1.
Days and hours180 days
SubjectsReading, Writing, Literature, Fine Arts, Math, Science, History, Geography, Government, Health, Physical Education
AssessmentAnnual nationally norm-referenced standardized test; results kept by the parent (not submitted).
Records to keepImmunization records with the notice; keep test results and instructional materials.
Parent qualificationNone.
Worth knowing180 days is a planning default only.

What HomeSchool Scribe does in Minnesota

  • Letter of intent: Prepares the notice letter; reminds you 14 days and 2 days before; lets you mark it filed with the date.
  • Annual standardized test: Prepares the transcript; reminds you 14 days and 2 days before; keeps it in records.

Attendance, hours, and grades fill in from the lessons you check off, so the documents above come from records you already have. Records are kept for 1 years, the period Minnesota asks for.
