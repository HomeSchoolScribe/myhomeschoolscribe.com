Homeschool requirements · Arizona

Homeschooling in Arizona

Light paperwork · A notice or an enrollment form, then keep your own records.

File a one-time affidavit of intent with the county school superintendent within 30 days of starting.

What Arizona asks

NoticeOne-time affidavit of intent (with a certified copy of the child's birth certificate) to the county school superintendent.
Days and hoursArizona sets no number. HomeSchool Scribe plans against 180 days unless you change it.
SubjectsReading, Grammar, Math, Social Studies, Science
AssessmentNone required.
Records to keepNo statutory record requirement; keep attendance and samples for your own files.
Parent qualificationNone.
Worth knowing180 days is a planning default only.

Affidavit of intent (one time)
Notice · Mail it		Once, when you beginCounty school superintendent for the county where the child livesArizona asks for this once, not every year, and it goes to the county rather than the state. It is due within 30 days of the day home instruction starts. The date here counts 30 days from your school year start, so if you began at another time of year, set your own reminder from that date. The affidavit asks for the child's full legal name as it appears on the birth certificate, the date of birth, the current address where school happens, and the names, phone numbers and addresses of the people who have custody. On the same 30-day clock, A.R.S. 15-828(B) asks for a certified copy of the child's birth certificate; a baptismal certificate, an application for a Social Security number, or original school registration records with an affidavit explaining why the birth certificate is unavailable are accepted instead. If that proof of identity is missing, the county writes to you and refers the case to local law enforcement if it does not arrive within 10 days. Maricopa and Pima counties also want the affidavit notarized and take it through their own online portals; other counties may differ, so check with yours. Easy to miss: the filing covers children of compulsory school age, six through sixteen; a child who is not eight by September 1 can wait, but only by filing an affidavit that says so; the county hears within 30 days if homeschooling stops; and a fresh affidavit goes in within 30 days if it later resumes. Children with an Empowerment Scholarship Account contract, or enrolled in a virtual charter or public-school-at-home program, do not file this one.
Nothing more to file: keep your own records
Records · Keep in your files		By the end of the school yearKeep in your filesAfter the affidavit, Arizona asks for nothing on a schedule. There is no required number of days or hours, no state testing for homeschooled children (A.R.S. 15-745), and no statutory record requirement. The law names five subjects for children six through sixteen: reading, grammar, mathematics, social studies and science. An attendance record and a course of study answer most questions if anyone ever asks, and the county's stamped copy of the affidavit is worth keeping for as long as you homeschool. The 180 days in the planner is a planning default, not an Arizona rule.
