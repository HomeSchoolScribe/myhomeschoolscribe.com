Homeschool requirements · South Dakota

Homeschooling in South Dakota

Light paperwork · A notice or an enrollment form, then keep your own records.

File a one-time notification (with a birth certificate) with the local district; annual testing was removed in 2021.

Alternative Instruction Notification
Notice · File online		Once, when you beginSouth Dakota Department of Education or your resident school districtFiled once for each child, within 30 days from the first time that child begins alternative instruction (SDCL 13-27-7, department FAQ 1.2 and 1.5), and not again each year. This reminder counts 30 days from the start date of this school year; if the child actually began on another date, move the due date to match, and skip it for a child who was notified in an earlier year. A separate notification goes in for each student. The paper form asks for the child's name, date of birth, resident district, an open-enrolled district if a nonresident district already accepted one, how the copy should come back by email or mail, and a parent signature; no curriculum is part of it, and FAQ 1.3 says a birth certificate or affidavit is not required for the notification or to provide alternative instruction. Either the department or the resident district can receive it, and the online system notifies both at once, though it opens behind a South Dakota state login. The paper form goes to the SD Department of Education, 800 Governors Drive, Pierre, SD 57501-2235, or to the school district's main office. A new notification goes in within 30 days if the child enrolls in a public or nonpublic school or moves to a different school district; the same form also covers correcting details, stopping alternative instruction, and moving out of state. For an 11th or 12th grader heading into the state's dual credit program, the department asks for the child's information to be updated in the notification system the semester before. HomeSchool Scribe prepares the details and opens the state's system; it does not file anything for you.
Nothing more to file: keep your own records
Records · Keep in your files		By the end of the school yearKeep in your filesAfter the notification South Dakota asks for nothing else: no day or hour count, no subjects beyond the basic skills of language arts and mathematics, no parent certification, and no standardized testing since Senate Bill 177 took effect on July 1, 2021 (department FAQ 4.1). The department recommends a detailed academic record of student learning, including attendance and grades earned, and says a parent-issued transcript is what activities participation, a later public school placement, the state's dual credit program, postsecondary admission, and scholarship applications ask for (FAQ 2.1). Nothing here has a deadline.
