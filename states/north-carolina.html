Homeschool requirements · North Carolina

Homeschooling in North Carolina

Moderate paperwork · A notice and a yearly assessment or records check.

File a one-time notice of intent with the Division of Non-Public Education, operate nine months a year, keep attendance and immunization records, and test annually.

What North Carolina asks

NoticeOne-time notice of intent to the NC Division of Non-Public Education before starting (parent needs a high school diploma or equivalent).
Days and hours180 days
SubjectsNot specified
AssessmentAnnual nationally standardized achievement test in English grammar, reading, spelling, and math; results kept for one year.
Records to keepAttendance records, immunization records, and test results.
Parent qualificationHigh school diploma or equivalent.
Worth knowingStatute requires a nine-month school term on a regular schedule; 180 days is a planning default.

What HomeSchool Scribe does in North Carolina

  • Notice of intent to DNPE: Prepares the notice letter; reminds you 14 days and 2 days before; opens the portal and keeps your confirmation.
  • Annual standardized test: Prepares the attendance and hours report and the transcript; reminds you 14 days and 2 days before; keeps it in records.

Attendance, hours, and grades fill in from the lessons you check off, so the documents above come from records you already have. Records are kept for 1 years, the period North Carolina asks for.
