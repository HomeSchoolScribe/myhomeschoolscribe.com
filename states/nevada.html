Homeschool requirements · Nevada

Homeschooling in Nevada

Light paperwork · A notice or an enrollment form, then keep your own records.

File a one-time notice of intent with an educational plan; no testing or reporting afterward.

What Nevada asks

NoticeOne-time notice of intent to the local school district (updated only if you move).
Days and hoursNevada sets no number. HomeSchool Scribe plans against 180 days unless you change it.
SubjectsEnglish, Math, Science, Social Studies

Records to keepKeep the educational plan; no reporting.

Notice of Intent to Homeschool
Notice · Mail it		Once, when you beginSuperintendent of the school district where the child livesFiled once, before the first day of homeschooling, and Nevada does not ask for it again year after year (NRS 388D.020; the state's own form calls it a one-time filing). Two other deadlines can replace that one: within 10 days after a child is formally withdrawn from a public school, or within 30 days after establishing residency in Nevada. One form per child, on the state's form, which asks for the child's full name, age and gender, the parents' names and address, a signed statement that you have control or charge of the child and the legal right to direct their education, the educational plan, and the most recently attended Nevada public school if there is one. Every district on the state's contact list takes the notice by mail and a few also take email; that list sits on the same state page as the form. Because this reminder does not repeat, set your own for a younger child reaching age 6, for a child returning to homeschooling after time in public school, and for a change to your name or address, which takes a new notice within 30 days. A complete notice has to be accepted as filed, and the district cannot ask for anything the statute does not list.
Keep the educational plan and the district's acknowledgment
Records · Keep in your files		By the end of the school yearKeep in your filesAfter the notice, Nevada asks for no reports and no testing, and sets no day or hour count, so nothing here carries a state deadline. What stays with you is the educational plan covering English language arts, mathematics, science and social studies including history, geography, economics and government, at the age and level of skill that fit the child; the law does not ask for every subject to be taught every year, and a parent may be asked to present the plan and proof of the child's identity to a court (NRS 388D.050). Keep the district's written acknowledgment with it, since that is the document that shows the child is legally homeschooled; the district holds its own copy for at least 15 years. The course of study document here is that plan, and the attendance report is for your own use, because the 180 days this app plans against is its default, not a Nevada rule.
