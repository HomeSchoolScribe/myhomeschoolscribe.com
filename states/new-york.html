Homeschool requirements · New York

Homeschooling in New York

Paperwork state · Several filings a year with real deadlines.

Submit an annual notice and an Individualized Home Instruction Plan (IHIP), file quarterly reports, log 900/990 hours over 180 days, and submit an annual assessment.

What New York asks

NoticeAnnual notice of intent by July 1 (or within 14 days of starting) followed by an IHIP for each child.
Days and hours180 days, 900 hours a year
SubjectsLanguage Arts, Math, Science, Social Studies, Health, Physical Education, Music, Visual Arts, Patriotism & Citizenship, Fire & Safety Education, Substance Abuse Prevention
AssessmentAnnual assessment: standardized test (required in grades 9–12 and every other year in 4–8) or written narrative evaluation in other years.
Records to keepQuarterly reports with hours and grades; attendance records; the IHIP.
Parent qualificationNone.
Worth knowing900 hours for grades 1–6, 990 hours for grades 7–12. Quarterly reports are due on the schedule set in your IHIP.

What HomeSchool Scribe does in New York

  • Letter of intent: Prepares the notice letter; reminds you 14 days and 2 days before; lets you mark it filed with the date.
  • IHIP (Individualized Home Instruction Plan): Prepares the course of study and the reading log; reminds you 14 days and 2 days before; lets you mark it filed with the date.
  • Quarterly report: Prepares the attendance and hours report and the report card; reminds you 14 days and 2 days before; lets you mark it filed with the date.
  • Annual assessment: Prepares the report card and the transcript; reminds you 14 days and 2 days before; lets you mark it filed with the date.

Attendance, hours, and grades fill in from the lessons you check off, so the documents above come from records you already have. Records are kept for 3 years, the period New York asks for.
