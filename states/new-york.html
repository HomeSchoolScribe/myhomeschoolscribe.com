Homeschool requirements · New York

Homeschooling in New York

Paperwork state · Several filings a year with real deadlines.

Submit an annual notice and an Individualized Home Instruction Plan (IHIP), file quarterly reports, log 900/990 hours over 180 days, and submit an annual assessment.

What New York asks

NoticeAnnual notice of intent by July 1 (or within 14 days of starting) followed by an IHIP for each child.
Days and hours180 days, 900 hours a year
SubjectsLanguage Arts (including reading, writing and spelling in grades 1–6), Math, Science, Social Studies (including geography and U.S. history), Health, Physical Education, Music, Visual Arts, U.S. and New York History and Constitutions (at least once in grades 1–8), Bilingual Education / English as a Second Language (grades 1–6, where needed), Practical Arts (grades 7–8), Library Skills (grades 7–8), Participation in Government (grades 9–12), Economics (grades 9–12), Electives (grades 9–12), Patriotism & Citizenship (K–12), Fire and Arson Prevention & Safety (K–12), Alcohol, Drug & Tobacco Misuse Prevention (K–12), Highway, Traffic & Bicycle Safety (K–12)
AssessmentAnnual assessment: use an approved standardized test each year in grades 9–12; in grades 4–8, a written narrative may be used no more often than every other school year; grades 1–3 may use a narrative each year. Choose a New York-certified teacher, home instruction peer review panel, or other person with the superintendent’s consent to write the narrative after interviewing the child and reviewing a portfolio. Home test administration also needs consent to the administrator. A test score is adequate if the composite is above the 33rd percentile or shows one academic year of growth against a test from the prior school year or later. An inadequate score or a narrative finding inadequate progress leads to probation and a plan to address the gaps. See 8 NYCRR 100.10(h)–(i) for approved tests and administration options.
Records to keepQuarterly reports with hours and grades; attendance records; the IHIP.
Parent qualificationNone.
Worth knowing900 hours for grades 1–6, 990 hours for grades 7–12 (each child’s own number is set at setup). Quarterly reports are due on the schedule set in your IHIP; one IHIP is filed per child, and the annual assessment is filed with the fourth report. Required subject units are totals across each grade band, not annual totals: 8 NYCRR 100.10 sets different lists for grades 1–6, 7–8, and 9–12. Across grades 7–8: 2 units each of English, history and geography, science, and math; 0.5 unit each of art and music; regular PE, health, practical arts, and library skills. Across grades 9–12: English 4; social studies 4 (including American history 1, participation in government 0.5, economics 0.5); math 2; science 2; art and/or music 1; health 0.5; PE 2; electives 3. One unit is 6,480 minutes of instruction. See 8 NYCRR 100.10(e) for the full grade-by-grade requirements.

What HomeSchool Scribe does in New York

  • Letter of intent: Prepares the notice letter; reminds you 14 days and 2 days before.
  • IHIP (Individualized Home Instruction Plan): Prepares the course of study and the reading log; reminds you 14 days and 2 days before.
  • Quarterly report: Prepares the attendance and hours report and the report card; reminds you 14 days and 2 days before.
  • Annual assessment: Prepares the report card and the transcript; reminds you 14 days and 2 days before.

Attendance, hours, and grades fill in from the lessons you check off, so the documents above come from records you already have. Records are kept for 3 years, the period New York asks for.
