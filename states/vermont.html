Homeschool requirements · Vermont

Homeschooling in Vermont

Moderate paperwork · A notice and a yearly assessment or records check.

Submit an enrollment notice with a minimum course of study each year and an end-of-year assessment.

What Vermont asks

NoticeAnnual enrollment notice to the Agency of Education.
Days and hours175 days
SubjectsReading, Writing, Math, Citizenship, History, U.S. & Vermont Government, Physical Education, Health, English, Literature, Science, Fine Arts
AssessmentYear-end assessment (portfolio review, standardized test, or teacher report) submitted to the state.
Records to keepPortfolio and assessment results.
Parent qualificationNone.

Home study enrollment notice
Notice · File online		Within -14 days of startingVermont Agency of Education, Home Study OfficeVermont enrolls home study through the Agency of Education, and the notice goes in every year. A family enrolling a child for the first time files the Notice of Intent; the same family files the Annual Notice in later years. Either way the notice carries an attestation that the child's progress is assessed each year, that the record of those assessments is kept, and that the child gets the equivalent of at least 175 days of instruction in the minimum course of study. The statute asks for the notice at least 10 business days before the program starts, so this reminder sits two weeks ahead of your school year start. Enrollment is not finished until the Agency sends written acknowledgment, which it aims to do within 10 business days of a complete form; the Agency says a student already at a public or independent school keeps attending until that enrollment is complete. Paper versions can be emailed to aoe.homestudy@vermont.gov, faxed to (802) 828-6433, or mailed to Home Study Office, 1 National Life Drive, Davis 5, Montpelier, VT 05620-2501. Leaving home study has its own written notice to the Agency, and the two sources word it differently: the Agency's page describes notice 10 days before a child returns to public or independent school, while the statute describes written notice within 10 business days after a child is withdrawn. This calendar cannot schedule that one for you.
Independent Professional Evidence
Notice · File online		Once, when you beginVermont Agency of Education, Home Study OfficeGoes with the first Notice of Intent for a child not previously enrolled in a Vermont public school or a Vermont home study program. The Agency's page says a licensed professional who is not related to the student fills it out, and the form addresses whether the student has a disability. The Agency also accepts a Vermont public school report card, an IEP or 504 plan, or other documentation of a disability in its place. Filed once for that child and not repeated in later years. There is a paper version as well, which goes to the Home Study Office by email, fax or mail like the rest of the enrollment paperwork. There is no document for the app to prepare here: a professional or a former school produces it.
Minimum course of study on file
Records · Keep in your files		When you start the school yearKeep in your own file; the Agency does not collect itYour district sets this date, not the state. HomeSchool Scribe has deliberately left the reminder off, because any date it picked would be invented. Confirm the date with your district or in your approved plan, then add your own reminder. The enrollment form asks for an attestation that a minimum course of study has been developed. Since Vermont's 2023 change the Agency no longer takes a copy, so the only copy is the one you keep. For a child under 13 the statute's fields are basic communication skills including reading, writing and the use of numbers; citizenship, history and government in Vermont and the United States; physical education and comprehensive health education; English, American and other literature; the natural sciences; and the fine arts. For a child 13 or older the list narrows to the first, second, fourth and fifth of those, so physical education and health and the fine arts are not on the older-student list. Vermont sets no date for having this written down; the app puts it at the start of your school year because that is when the attestation is made. The app can print a course of study from your courses whenever someone asks to see it.
175 days of instruction
Records · Keep in your files		By the end of the school yearKeep in your own file; the Agency does not collect itVermont asks for the equivalent of at least 175 days of instruction in the minimum course of study each year. The statute sets no hours-per-day figure and no calendar date for the count; the app checks in at the end of your school year. Nothing is sent in, but the attendance record is what stands behind the attestation on the next enrollment notice. Checking lessons off keeps the day count current, so the report is ready if the Agency or a district ever asks.
End-of-year assessment
Assessment · Keep in your files		By the end of the school yearKeep in your own file; the Agency does not collect itEach child's progress is assessed at the end of the school year and the record stays with the family. The statute lists five permitted ways: a standardized assessment; a review of progress by someone holding a current Vermont teacher's certificate; a parent report and portfolio with a summary of what the child learned plus at least four samples of the child's work; grades from an online academy or school; or evidence of passing the GED. Since Vermont's 2023 change the Agency no longer requires this to be sent in. The next enrollment notice asks for an attestation that this record was kept, so a missing assessment shows up a year later.
Keep the home study file
Records · Keep in your files		By the end of the school yearKeep in your own fileThe statute says the parent or guardian maintains the record of each year's assessments and names no number of years. This file is set to three years because Vermont is silent, which also covers the attestation each enrollment notice asks for. Worth keeping alongside it: the Agency's acknowledgment letters, the attendance record behind the 175 days, and the course of study. Confirm the Agency's current practice if a firm retention period matters to you.
